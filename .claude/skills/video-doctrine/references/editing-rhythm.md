# Editing Rhythm

A short film needs an arc, not N seconds of equally-distributed activity. v1 measured
**escalation 0.62** — it ends at under two thirds the energy it opened with. It does not
build; it runs down.

## The arc, for 25 seconds

| time    | job                                        |
| ------- | ------------------------------------------ |
| 0-4s    | sparse, slow discovery. One signal moving  |
| 4-10s   | movement increases, the system forms       |
| 10-17s  | accelerated transformation                 |
| 17-21s  | ONE clear payoff, held                     |
| 21-25s  | recognition and exit                       |

## Accelerating cut rate

Long shots first, shortening: 4s → 3s → 2s → 1.2s → micro-cuts of 6-12 frames → a 2-3s
final hold. This creates escalation without adding a single word.

## Cut on the beat, but not every beat

Hit structural changes on major beats; use off-beat micro-events for anticipation; let
motion occasionally lead or lag the sound. Reserve the strongest beat for the brand
landing. Cutting on every beat is predictable, which is its own kind of flat.

Our audio kit derives its hits from `cutsheet.json`, so picture and score cannot drift.

## The hold

A deliberate pause after an important event. Use it after a transformation, a first
product reveal, a mark forming, a before/after change.

**A hold is not dead time; it is when meaning lands.** The common mistake is to keep
animating because silence feels empty.

**Measuring it is harder than it looks.** Our `holds` metric is explicitly UNCALIBRATED:
in a caption-driven film a word appearing IS an energy spike and the word then sitting
still IS a pause, so a slideshow scores as held. Two mechanism changes failed to separate
them. Read `escalation` instead until `holds` has agreed with the operator's eye once.

## The value of one long take

A single continuous move makes causality visible: camera passes a dormant node → the node
activates → its signal routes → the system reorganises → the result is the product. The
audience sees cause and effect rather than a series of claims. That is D3's entire premise.

## Hard cut vs transition

Cut for a new emotional state, a shock, a reset, a scale change, a before/after.
Transition for continuity. **Use a cut when you want interpretation.**
